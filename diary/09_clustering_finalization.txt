WHAT WE WORKED ON THIS WEEK

This week we completed the modeling notebook. Two main tasks: finalize
the classifier comparison and add the meeting-level clustering.

CLASSIFIER FINALIZATION
We ran all five CV experiments to completion and recorded macro-F1 and
accuracy for each model. The full results are documented in the
modeling notebook. XGBoost came out on top by macro-F1, followed
closely by Random Forest. The two linear models (LR and SVM) were
competitive on the majority class (discussion) but weaker on the
minority classes (decision and action), which is expected — linear
decision boundaries struggle with the subtle language patterns that
distinguish a casual agreement from a formal decision.

The error analysis was eye-opening. The most common confusion is
between action items and discussion. Looking at the misclassified
examples, we found that many action-item utterances in the AMI corpus
are phrased very informally ("I can look into that", "maybe we should
try") without the explicit ownership or deadline cues our wrangling
lexicon looks for. This suggests that expanding the action lexicon in
a future iteration would improve both the labeling step and the
classifier's training signal.

MEETING-LEVEL CLUSTERING
The utterance classifier answers "what kind of utterance is this?"
The clustering answers "what kind of meeting is this?" We ran K-Means
on four meeting-level features: decision density, action density,
redundancy score, and action clarity. All features were standardized
before clustering.

To select k, we plotted both the elbow curve (inertia vs. k) and the
silhouette score (cluster cohesion vs. k). The silhouette score peaked
at k=4, which also corresponds to a visible elbow in the inertia curve.
Four clusters gave us interpretable groups that we labeled from most to
least efficient based on average efficiency score: Highly Efficient,
Moderately Efficient, Low Efficiency, and Very Low Efficiency.

A 2D PCA projection confirmed the clusters are reasonably well
separated in the reduced space. The Highly Efficient cluster is a
small group of meetings with both high decision density and high action
clarity — these appear to be scenario meetings from the AMI corpus
where participants were given a more structured task. The majority of
meetings fall into the Low Efficiency and Moderately Efficient clusters.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISIONS MADE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

- k=4 for K-Means, selected by silhouette score.
- Cluster labels assigned by ranking clusters on average efficiency
  score so that the naming is intuitive and data-driven, not arbitrary.
- Save the full pipeline (TF-IDF + classifier) together as one pickle
  so inference in Milestone 3 requires only one .predict() call.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
SETBACKS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

We initially tried k=3 clusters, which worked but merged the two
bottom tiers into one group that was hard to interpret. Silhouette
analysis confirmed k=4 was stronger, and manually reviewing the
meetings in each cluster confirmed the distinction is meaningful.
