WHAT WE WORKED ON THIS WEEK
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

This week we finished the wrangling pipeline by implementing the three
core efficiency metrics and adding a validation layer.

DECISION DENSITY
Defined as the number of decision-labeled utterances divided by total
utterances in a meeting. This is simple to compute and easy to
interpret: a meeting with density 0.05 made a decision roughly once
every 20 utterances. Across the 171 AMI meetings, the average is low —
most meetings are dominated by general discussion, which matches what
we expected from real workplace settings.

REDUNDANCY SCORE
This was the most technically involved metric. The idea is to measure
how much of what gets said has already been said earlier in the same
meeting. We used bigram TF-IDF cosine similarity: for each utterance,
we compare its bigram profile against all previous utterances in the
meeting and flag it as redundant if the maximum similarity exceeds a
threshold of 0.30. The redundancy score for a meeting is the fraction
of utterances that exceed this threshold.

We chose bigrams over unigrams because individual words repeat
constantly in any conversation ("the", "we", "meeting") but bigram
combinations are more distinctive. The 0.30 threshold was chosen by
manually reviewing a sample of flagged pairs and adjusting until the
flags looked semantically reasonable.

ACTION CLARITY
Defined as the fraction of action-labeled utterances that contain an
explicit owner (e.g. "I will", "you will") or an explicit deadline
(e.g. "by Friday", "before next week", "by EOD"). This captures
whether action items are properly assigned or just vaguely mentioned.
Meetings with low action clarity may assign tasks without naming
who is responsible, which is a known cause of follow-through failure.

EFFICIENCY SCORE
A composite of the three metrics: 0.4 * decision_density +
0.3 * action_density + 0.3 * (1 - redundancy_score). The weights
were chosen to prioritize decisions slightly over action items, and
to penalize high redundancy. All three underlying metrics are in [0,1]
so the composite is also in [0,1] and can be interpreted directly.

VALIDATION
We added assertion-based checks at the end of the wrangling notebook:
- All metric columns are in [0, 1]
- No null values in any required column
- Schema matches the expected column list

All checks passed. The final output is analysis_ready.csv (utterance
level, ~134k rows) and meeting_metrics.csv (meeting level, 171 rows).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISIONS MADE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

- Bigram cosine similarity at threshold 0.30 for redundancy detection.
- Composite efficiency formula with weights 0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3.
- Hard validation assertions at the end of the pipeline so future
  changes cannot silently break the schema.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
SETBACKS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Computing redundancy for all 171 meetings using cosine similarity was
slow the first time we ran it (about 12 minutes on a laptop). We
optimized it by using scikit-learn's CountVectorizer with ngram_range
(2,2) and sparse matrix operations instead of looping over pairs
manually. After optimization, runtime dropped to under 2 minutes.
