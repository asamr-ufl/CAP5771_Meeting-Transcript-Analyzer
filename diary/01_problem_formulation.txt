=== PROBLEM FORMULATION ===

1. PROBLEM TYPE
We are solving multiple interconnected problems:
- Classification: Classify sentences as "decision," "action item," or "general discussion"
- Exploration: Discover patterns like decision timing and redundancy
- Decision Support: Help managers assess meeting effectiveness

2. CENTRAL QUESTION
Are meetings actually producing decisions and clear action items, or are they mostly 
repetitive discussion?

3. WHY THIS MATTERS
- Meetings consume significant time in professional/academic environments
- Poor meetings waste labor hours and create unclear responsibilities
- Objective measurement of meeting effectiveness is rarely done
- Transcripts already exist, making this analysis feasible

4. DATA SOURCES
- AMI Meeting Corpus (manual annotations, 22MB)
- ICSI Meeting Corpus
- Both are publicly available and structured for research

5. WHAT MAKES THIS UNIQUE
- Focuses on information quality, not sentiment analysis
- Produces interpretable metrics (decision density, redundancy score, action item clarity)
- Output is actionable, not a black-box score

6. REQUIRED VARIABLES
- Text of each utterance: Needed to analyze content
- Meeting ID: To group utterances into meetings
- Speaker turns/timestamps: To track sequence and repetition
These enable measurement of decision timing, redundancy, and follow-through.

7. SUCCESS CRITERIA
A meaningful output shows:
- Low decision density → Few decisions made
- High redundancy → Repetitive discussion
- Poorly defined action items → Unclear next steps
This clearly indicates an inefficient meeting.

8. STAKEHOLDERS & BENEFITS
- Team leads, instructors, and managers
- Can identify meetings needing restructuring
- Improves productivity without monitoring individuals

9. REALISTIC SCOPE
- Dataset is already available
- Analysis uses standard NLP tools on a laptop
- Streamlit interface sufficient for demonstration
- Timeline: 10 weeks (achievable for 3-person team)

10. HEILMEIER CATECHISM SUMMARY
What are we trying to do? Measure if meetings result in decisions vs. repetitive discussion.
How is it done today? Subjectively through opinions/surveys (inconsistent, often ignored).
What's new? Direct analysis of what was said using existing transcripts for objective measurement.
Who cares? Managers/teams/instructors who want better meetings to save time.
Risks? Misclassifying statements; domain differences (acceptable, results are explanatory not punitive).
Cost? Zero monetary cost; only computation and development time.
Timeline? One semester; core prototype within 6-8 weeks.
Mid-term success? Core metrics (decision density, redundancy, action item clarity) correctly 
computed for subset of meetings with reasonable, explainable results.
Final success? Fully functional Streamlit app that processes meeting transcripts and provides 
clear visual feedback on meeting efficiency for previously unseen transcripts.
